The decision, in one paragraph

Tenarai has no legal entity, no office and no client of record in South Korea, while its five stated core industries — technology, retail/CPG, travel & hospitality, healthcare, and telecom/manufacturing — map almost one-for-one onto the Korean conglomerate base that is currently the most active enterprise-AI buyer in Asia (source 1 §1, §2.7). Under Korean law a foreign company can operate in only three forms, and only one of them can sign a contract, issue a tax invoice or register as an enterprise vendor: a foreign-invested local subsidiary (source 1 §4; src-a §5.1; src-b §02). Formation is therefore not a growth option among several — it is the minimum condition for participating at all.

What the board is being asked to approve is small and bounded: a single capitalisation of $705K covering setup plus 24 months of runway, against a maximum cumulative group outlay of $1.39M that ends permanently in Year 6, returning a 38.0% IRR and 7.5× on funds committed in the base case (src-d, recommendation and §3, restated 2026-08-13 on the revised office-rent schedule per §10.2). The downside case — half the revenue, no anchor accounts, hiring frozen at the Year 3 gate — still returns 4.9× and a positive NPV (src-d §7).

Recommendation

Approve establishment of a wholly-owned Korean subsidiary (주식회사 / joint-stock company) under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, with a single paid-in capitalisation of $705K (₩1,008M), conditional on the transfer-pricing structure being confirmed as the principal model before capital is paid in (source 1 §9.1; src-a §7; src-d recommendation).

The five arguments, in the order a board tests them

  1. 1

    The commitment is capped, and the cap is small.

    Cumulative HQ outlay peaks at $1.39M at end-Year 6 and then goes to zero — the subsidiary is self-funding from Year 7. Closing at end-Year 4, after four full years of evidence, costs $1.13M before recovering the $35K office deposit and residual paid-in capital (src-d §2.1, §5).

  2. 2

    Asset-light by construction, so the cost base is reversible.

    Engineering and delivery stay with the parent's India hubs. The Korean entity carries sales, presales and delivery management only — no bench, no data centre, no capitalised development. Every cost line is headcount, rent or travel, so a decision to stop is executable within one quarter (src-d §2.2; src-c §1-3).

  3. 3

    The funding requirement is small, dated, and ends.

    HQ funds an annual net gap of $20K–$319K across Years 1–6 and nothing thereafter — an operating-budget decision, not a capital raise (src-d §2.3, §3).

  4. 4

    The demand is appropriated, not forecast.

    Korean financial institutions have already tendered an estimated $1.4B of core-system replacement work for 2026–2027, including the largest single financial-sector IT contract in Korean history (NH NongHyup Bank "NEO" Phase 1, $213.6M / ₩305.5B). This is budget released through public tender, not an analyst forecast (src-c §2-2; src-d §2.4, §4).

  5. 5

    The return clears the hurdle with room to spare.

    38.0% IRR and $3.11M NPV at a 12% discount rate, against a market-entry hurdle typically underwritten at 15–20%. It still returns 31.7% if group gross margin lands three points below plan (src-d §7).

Three things this report deliberately does not claim

Tax incentives are not part of the case

The commonly cited "5 years 100% + 2 years 50%" corporate tax exemption for foreign-invested companies was abolished for applications from 2019 onward. A Tenarai Korea incorporated in 2026 cannot receive it (source 1 §6.1). Entry timing must not be justified on tax grounds — see section 7.

Entity formation does not produce a pipeline

It creates the legal ability to sell. Local sales leadership, reference accounts and brand-building are separate, separately funded workstreams (src-a §6; src-e §1).

Korea's largest IT investment pools are out of scope

Semiconductor manufacturing and automotive are not among Tenarai's core industries, and Wipro holds that ground through its HARMAN DTS acquisition. This is excluded by decision, not solved by entry (src-a §4.3; src-c §7; src-d §8).

5.2 The verdict — subsidiary

Recommendation: establish a foreign-invested local subsidiary (주식회사 / joint-stock company). The reasoning, not just the table (source 1 §4.1; src-a §5.3; src-b §02).

1

A liaison office is not an option at all

Tenarai's model — signing consulting contracts, invoicing, hiring staff, contracting directly with clients — is unambiguously "business activity". A liaison office cannot sign a contract or issue a Korean tax invoice, which means it cannot generate a single dollar of revenue (source 1 §4.1; src-a §5.2).

2

A branch does not separate risk

Its legal personality is subordinate to HQ, exposing the parent directly to Korean litigation and liabilities, and it can raise branch-profit-tax issues on Korean-source income (source 1 §4.1).

3

The tax difference is not material

Between branch and subsidiary (see section 7) — so "the branch is cheaper on tax" is not a defensible reason to choose one (source 1 §4.1).

4

The subsidiary is the form Korean buyers expect

Registering as a vendor with a Korean conglomerate, or bidding on public-sector procurement through KONEPS, requires a Korean business registration number — obtainable only by a branch or a subsidiary — and a domestic entity as counterparty is materially more favourable in vendor screening (src-a §5.2; src-c §6-1).

8.3 PIPA cross-border transfer — the structural issue

Separate consent per purpose

PIPA Article 28-8 requires separate consent, or another lawful basis, for each cross-border transfer purpose. Consent obtained for one purpose does not extend to another.

A Korean-language policy, not a translation

A Korean-language privacy policy carrying a distinct overseas-provision section is required. A translated global policy is not sufficient.

No shortcut exists

As of mid-2026, neither an adequacy decision nor a certification pathway for cross-border transfer is operational — so the transfer must rest on consent or another explicit lawful basis.

Buyers audit this before signing

Korean enterprise customers now routinely audit vendors' data-processing terms, breach-notification procedures, security controls, overseas-transfer details and subcontractor lists before signing.

12.11 The decision, stated plainly

The question is not whether Tenarai can build a $35 million business in Korea in ten years.

It is whether $705,000 and an annual budget line are worth committing to find out.

They are. The base case returns 38.0% on a maximum group outlay of $1.39M that ends permanently in Year 6. The downside — half the revenue, no anchor accounts, hiring held at the Year 3 level under the gate — still returns 4.9× and a positive $2.15M of present value, provided that gate is actually enforced rather than waived. The accelerated case, which requires only that the subsidiary participate at all in the $1.4bn of demand already tendered in its first two years, cuts the outlay by close to half and lifts the return to 50%.

Two structural facts make this unusual. The subsidiary is asset-light, so headquarters can stop the cost base rather than unwind it. And the demand is appropriated rather than forecast, so the market risk is one of competitive access, not of market existence. Those two together are what make a bounded commitment rational here where it would not be for a market entry that had to build local delivery against a projected market.

Recommendation: approve establishment of the Korean subsidiary and a single capitalisation of $705,000 (₩1,008 million), covering setup cost plus a 24-month runway, subject to the transfer-pricing structure being confirmed as the principal model before the capital is paid in. Fund Years 3–5 through one further capital increase at the Year 3 gate. Control the commitment through annual operating-budget approval, and treat the Year 3 hiring gate as a binding governance commitment, not a passing mention — the downside case's return depends on it firing (src-d, conclusion).

13.2 Three execution risks specific to the market-entry marketing programme

Carried separately because they are campaign-level rather than investment-level, and because two of them have no financial mitigation — only a rule (src-e §13).

The Month-20 brand burst lands before the entity can contract

The highest-probability failure mode. Month 20 is a floor, not a fixture. If vendor registrations or the first two references slip, the burst slips with them. Media contracts must be signed with that flexibility priced in.

Creative misfires culturally

The "legacy obituary" execution in particular, since obituary formats carry real cultural weight in Korea. Korean creative sign-off with a named approver; the Korean advisory board member reviews all five executions before production. It ships only if that approval is unambiguous.

Community seeding is read as astroturfing

Korean professional communities punish this severely and permanently, and a foreign brand caught doing it loses more than the campaign. Rule: Tenarai employees post under their real names with disclosure, or Tenarai does not post. No exceptions.

Self-reported performance claims get challenged

Tenarai's global figures ("80% time reduction", "3–4× throughput") are unvalidated by third parties, and Korean enterprise buyers and trade press treat unsourced vendor numbers harshly. Apply the copy rule without exception: in Korea these appear only with a named client and a stated measurement basis, or not at all.

13.3 What formation does not solve — the honest closing

Forming the entity creates the legal ability to sell. It does not, by itself, produce a sales pipeline. Local sales leadership, reference accounts and brand-building remain separate workstreams that should be scoped alongside entity formation, not treated as automatic outputs of it (src-a §6).
