01 · SUMMARY

Key conclusions

  • The plan assumes a locally incorporated subsidiary (jusik hoesa, a Korean joint-stock company). Under the Enforcement Decree of the Foreign Investment Promotion Act art. 2(2), investment qualifies as foreign direct investment only if it is at least KRW 100 million (approx. $69,930) and confers at least 10% of voting shares.
  • Setup cost is estimated at $43,944 (including a $34,965 office deposit and $4,895 in equipment). The source material assumed roughly $1,399 in incorporation-related fees; that understates the cost for a Seoul-based entity, because it omits the threefold surcharge on registration license tax that applies inside the Seoul metropolitan concentration control region.
  • The five-year cumulative requirement is $1,805,993 ($1.81M) under the base case, which escalates payroll, office and other operating costs by 20% per year.
  • In practice the full five years are not funded up front. We recommend securing a 24-month runway of $564,867 ($564.9K) as the initial capital injection, with funding from year 3 onward gated on headquarters approval against year 1–2 performance.
  • These figures are gross cost — no revenue is netted off. Once the subsidiary books revenue, the actual net cash injection required will be lower.

02 · ENTITY TYPE

Choosing the entity type

03 · PROCESS

Procedure and timeline

An entity funded by a foreign investor follows the standard Korean incorporation process plus two additional steps: the foreign investment notification at the front and the FDI company registration at the end. Including document preparation, allow approximately two to three months.

StepActionAuthorityDuration
1Foreign investment notification — may be filed before funds moveKOTRA (Invest Korea) or a foreign exchange banksame day–1 day
2Remittance of investment funds from HQ to a temporary accountForeign exchange bank1–5 days
3Incorporation registration — articles of incorporation, director acceptancesCommercial registry office1–2 weeks
4Corporate establishment filing and business registrationDistrict tax office1–2 weeks
5Transfer of paid-in capital from temporary to corporate accountForeign exchange bank1–2 days
6FDI company registration — within 60 days of completing the investmentSame body that received the notification1–2 weeks
  • The 60-day registration deadline is mandatory. Under Foreign Investment Promotion Act art. 21(1), registration must be filed within 60 days of the triggering event (typically completion of the capital payment). Missing it exposes the company to an administrative fine, and any D-8 visa application slips with it.
  • The notification and the registration must go through the same institution — if the notification was filed with KOTRA, register with KOTRA.
  • The FDI registration certificate is a prerequisite for the D-8 (corporate investment) visa. If expatriate staff are to be posted, work the staffing schedule backwards from the registration completion date.

04 · DOCUMENTS

Required documents

Issued in the United States — apostille required

The United States is a party to the Apostille Convention, so a state-level apostille replaces consular legalisation. All three documents must be accompanied by a notarised Korean translation.

DocumentFormNote
Proof of corporate existenceCertificate of Incorporation or Certificate of Good StandingEvidences legal name and registered address
Proof of signing authorityCertificate of Incumbency or board resolutionMust match the signatory on the power of attorney
Power of attorneyGrants a Korean agent authority to invest and registerRequired if the investor does not travel to Korea
Signature certificateNotarised specimen signatureSubstitutes for the Korean seal certificate

The single most common cause of rejection is an inconsistent corporate name. "Tenarai Inc." and "Tenarai, Inc." — a difference of one comma — will draw a correction order from the registry office. Reconcile the legal name character by character across the US documents, the articles of incorporation and the notification form before the apostille is obtained; re-notarisation adds another two to three weeks.

Prepared in Korea

  • Foreign investment notification form
  • Articles of incorporation — notarisation is waived where paid-in capital is under KRW 1 billion and the company is formed by promoter incorporation (Commercial Act art. 292, proviso)
  • Director acceptance letters and seal or signature certificates
  • Office lease agreement — required for business registration
  • Foreign currency purchase / deposit certificate evidencing the inbound remittance
  • Shareholder register
  • Corporate seal registration and seal card issuance

Begin preparing the US documents at least one to two months before the target incorporation date, to absorb apostille and translation lead time.

06 · FIVE-YEAR PLAN

Five-year funding requirement

Basis and assumptions

  • Escalation: payroll, office, other operating costs and sales costs all escalate at 20% per year (growth case). This is not price inflation — it should be read as headcount growth, office expansion and wider sales activity combined. Wage inflation alone does not account for 20% a year.
  • Two sales staff assumed — general sales expense of KRW 300k per head per month.
  • The KRW 5 million monthly "statutory and administrative costs" line is taken to already include the employer share of the four social insurances, retirement pension accrual and bookkeeping fees, so no separate severance line is carried.
  • Country manager / expatriate salary is excluded (nil), consistent with the source data — see risk 3 below.
  • Setup cost falls in year 1 only. The office security deposit is a recoverable asset and is treated as a cash requirement, not an expense.
  • No revenue is netted off; these are gross costs.

07 · RISKS

Financial implications and open points

1. The five years are not funded in one tranche

A new entity is normally funded to a runway of 18–24 months, until revenue stabilises. On a 24-month basis the requirement is $564,867 ($564.9K). Funding from year 3 onward is more realistically gated on headquarters approval against year 1–2 results.

2. Paid-in capital and working capital are separate questions

The statutory FDI minimum is KRW 100 million, but a thinly capitalised entity fares poorly in D-8 visa assessment and in counterparty credit checks. Conversely, higher capital increases the 1.2% registration license tax proportionally. Starting at around KRW 300 million and funding the balance through an HQ loan or a later capital increase is worth considering.

3. A nil country-manager salary is a credibility risk

A business plan showing zero compensation for the country manager invites questions about sustainability at investment-committee review. Adding a minimum expatriate salary line (on the order of KRW 50–60 million a year) would raise the five-year total by roughly $286,217 under the base case (a KRW 55M base escalated at 20%). This report preserves the original assumption, but the figure should be included in any version circulated outside the company.

4. State what the 20% escalation actually represents

Raising payroll 20% a year means adding people, not raising salaries. Year 5 payroll of $311,762 cannot be explained by the initial three-person team, so the plan needs an accompanying headcount schedule — otherwise headquarters will ask for the derivation.

5. Sales costs scale non-linearly

Once senior sales staff on six-figure packages start producing, corporate card, entertainment and petty cash spend will substantially exceed the KRW 300k per month assumption. Without a separately designed incentive pool, the sales cost line is where the budget breaks first.
